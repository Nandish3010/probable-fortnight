The problem · how big, which rule, who decides

The forecast sees the write-off coming, but nothing acts on it.

How big · as reported
1.8%
of net order value (NOV) is lost to inventory at Blinkit.

As reported by Inc42, 22 Jul 2026. Per-store line below shows its inputs, from the same article.

₹17,132 cr ÷ 2,443 stores × 4 × 1.8% ≈ ₹0.5 cr per store a year

All inventory loss, one quarter annualised. Context for scale, not a Taal result.

The rule · an advisory, not law
30% or 45 days
of shelf life must remain at delivery of an online order.

FSSAI advisory to e-commerce food sellers, 3 Dec 2024. Stock can be in date on the shelf yet past its online sell-by, so the deadline can come weeks before expiry.

Either-limit reading is Taal's configurable default.

Who it is for

A category or supply-chain head

They own write-off and are the buyer. Day to day, two people use Taal:

  • A demand planner checks the gap and the guardrails, then approves the play on the Play Desk.
  • A dark-store manager confirms what is on the pallet on the phone view and receives any transfer.

List of features

Six capabilities, gated by rules a model never touches.

Vision intake

Photograph a pallet; dates and counts come back with confidence. Low confidence goes to a person.

Nightly Sense

Per-SKU, per-node forecasts. Six gap types, each with rupees and a deadline.

Planner Agent

ADK LoopAgent, max 3 iterations, six deterministic tools; a Cost Governor picks the gaps.

Approve + assignment

A human approves. Holdout by hash; offers go to treated customers only.

Customer Agent

Grounded in stock, offers and consent. Orders through an in-process MCP tool.

Measure

Treated vs holdout, 95% CI. Priors update from the counts.

User guide

Three people, three screens, one approved play.

Planner

/desk
  1. 1Open the Play Desk and pick a gap.
  2. 2Check the guardrails and counterfactuals.
  3. 3Click Approve. The forecast moves.

Node manager

/phone
  1. 1Photograph a pallet, or tap a sample.
  2. 2Confirm the rows read below 0.7 confidence.
  3. 3Approve the play from the gap card.

Customer

/chat
  1. 1Open the chat. It starts with "Any offers today?".
  2. 2Ask about a product. You see a stock band, not a count.
  3. 3Send STOP to withdraw consent.
Badges. LIVE: a real call this visit. REPLAY: stored output replayed. SYNTHETIC: fabricated demo data. "Recorded from Gemini": a dated real model run.

Longevity · running it for real

What it takes to run beyond the demo.

Production path

  • Today: services run as the owner-role deploy identity; CI uses a stored key. Secrets sit in a managed store.
  • Next: least-privilege service accounts and workload identity.
  • Model IDs pinned with retirement dates: gemini-3.5-flash live, Flash-Lite fallback; the previous model retires 20 Oct 2026.

Scalability

  • Measured: Sense 3.8 s (local, 300 SKUs); chat p50 3.66 s, p95 4.36 s (14 Oct, gemini-3.5-flash, Model Armor on, local API); live plan p50 26.5 s, p95 31.8 s (22/24, 11 Oct); Approve 1.4 to 1.7 s warm (13 Oct; first call after idle takes longer).
  • Estimate: 20,000 SKUs, 500 nodes.estimate
  • Breaks first: max-instances 1 with re-plan state in memory, then the planner queue.

Feasibility

  • Buyer: category or supply-chain head. Fee per node or rupee share.
  • Pilot: 50 to 60 testers, one week, not yet run.
  • Run cost: ₹486 a month, usage measured 23 Sep on gemini-2.5-flash, not a bill.
  • Estimates: ₹200 to 500 (Micro), ₹2,500 to 5,000 (Mid), ₹1 to 2 lakh (Large).estimate
